## Decisions (T031 left them to T032)

1. **A read allowed again after a refusal** is `blocked` with reason `flapping`, Lower up to that
   read, no Upper: the refusal did not end the window, and reporting it as one would understate
   how long a deleted client's token works (C007.5). Observe therefore makes `Confirm` = 3 reads
   (90 s at the default interval) after a refusal before the expiry, still within the expiry
   plus one interval. A refusal at or after the expiry ends polling at once (T031's
   valid-until-expiry case requires that). Whether OVHcloud flaps is UNVERIFIED (T026 observes).
2. **A read allowed exactly at the expiry instant** is within the lifetime: `expires_in` is whole
   seconds, so at the instant either answer is consistent, as T031 already fixed for a refusal
   there. One second later it is `blocked (expiry)`.
3. **read_path** must be exactly `/cloud/project/<project_id>`: the policy allows only
   `publicCloudProject:apiovh:get` (kb api/v1/cloud.json, GET /cloud/project/{serviceName}), so a
   path below the project would be refused and read as `blocked (control)`. T031's Deviations
   admitted paths below it; its tests admit no other (all refusal rows still refused).
4. **Reasons added** to the set: `flapping` (ResidualWindow), and in Observe's record `setup`
   (client, policy or token not obtained) and `delete` (the client delete failed: no window).
   data-model.md says so.
5. **Times** (revised in review round 1): the record keeps `requested_at` (token request sent)
   and `issued_at` (its answer arrived); the token's real expiry lies between them plus
   `expires_in`. ResidualWindow judges against the later edge, so an allowed read after it outlived
   the token for certain; a refusal counts as before the expiry only if its answer
   (`answered_at`) came back before the earlier edge, else it is judged as a refusal at the expiry
   (valid-until-expiry when the poll before it was allowed, with Upper from its answer, else
   blocked). Reads and control retries are scheduled at absolute instants (the previous send plus
   one interval), so a read's latency never pushes the next one past the expiry plus one
   interval or past Settle; a read slower than the interval is followed by a check of the clock
   before the next send (review round 2). Upper runs from the delete's request
   (`delete_requested_at`) to the refusal's answer, the edges that cannot understate it; Lower
   from the delete's acknowledgement (`deleted_at`) to the allowed read's send, the edges that
   cannot overstate it (review round 2). Whether OVHcloud truncates or rounds `expires_in` is
   UNVERIFIED: an allowed read within a second after the stated expiry is `blocked (expiry)`, as
   T031's allowed-past-expiry case requires (see Review round 2).
6. **No bind**: the check never binds its client (`Options.Bind` stays unused here): a bind takes a
   token of its own, and the claim is about exactly one token.
7. **Project URN region** comes from the created client's identity URN (`urn:v1:<region>:…`,
   shape-gated); tools/internal/stacks writes the same `urn:v1:<region>:resource:publicCloudProject:<id>`.

## Learnings

Class: sensor.
- A probe that times a window from its own clock owns two edges per event (request sent, answer
  arrived): pick, per claim, the edge that cannot overstate it, record both, and give the fake a
  transport that advances the clock before and after the server sees a request.
- A schedule bound ("no poll later than X") needs the clock read again just before each send, and
  a case whose latency exceeds the interval; a latency below the interval hides it.
- A mutant that removes a latency fix survives unless the latency puts an event inside the window
  where the overrun shows; compute the alignment, do not pick a round number.
